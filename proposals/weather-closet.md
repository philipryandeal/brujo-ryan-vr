# Scene Proposal — The Weather Closet

**Working title:** The Weather Closet  
**Contributor:** Seraph Nganga  
**Status:** Proposal — not yet built or connected  
**Suggested adjacency:** The Department of Unnecessary Doors, if Ryan and the steward approve a real passage later.

## Entrance

A narrow utility door in the Department of Unnecessary Doors is labeled:

**WEATHER — STORAGE ONLY  
NO CLIMATE REQUESTS  
YES, THIS MEANS YOU**

Nothing speaks when the visitor arrives.

Beyond the door is a room much larger than the door has any business containing. Tall dark-green shelving disappears upward into mist. Hundreds of jars, bottles, tins, folded paper packets and stoppered ceramic crocks hold small pieces of weather: a thumb-sized thunderhead, three inches of seaside fog, one very annoyed gust of wind, a jar of rain that falls upward only when nobody is touching the shelf.

The floor is old stone with shallow drainage channels cut through it for reasons that become obvious very quickly.

## Setting

The room is warm near the entrance and progressively stranger toward the shelves.

Orange utility lamps hang from crooked iron hooks. Their light catches condensation on the bottles. A ceiling cannot quite be seen; only rafters, mist and the occasional flicker of distant sheet lightning.

Some containers are neatly labeled:

- **DRIZZLE — POLITE**
- **DRIZZLE — PERSISTENT**
- **DRIZZLE — EMOTIONALLY COMPLICATED**
- **WIND, EAST — RETURN TO TREE & LEAF**
- **SUNBEAM, DOMESTIC — 14 MINUTES**
- **THUNDER, THEATRICAL — DO NOT SHAKE**
- **SNOW — WRONG SEASON / NOT OUR FAULT**
- **TUESDAY, 4:17 PM**

A little brass floor drain sighs every few minutes.

The room sounds like rain heard from another room in a house.

## Resident

### Mizzle

Mizzle is a short goblin in a waxed apron, rubber boots two sizes too large and a battered cap with a pencil tucked behind one ear.

Mizzle is the **Custodian of Atmospheric Misplacement**.

This is not a ceremonial title. Weather gets left everywhere.

People open windows. Storms wander. Fog takes shortcuts. Children bring lightning bugs indoors and sometimes the lightning follows them. Mizzle collects what does not belong where it ended up, labels it as accurately as possible, and sends it home when a suitable return passage presents itself.

Mizzle was doing this before the visitor arrived and will continue after they leave.

Mizzle dislikes being called a weather wizard.

"Wizard" implies somebody planned this.

## Discoveries

### 1. Mizzle

Clicking or selecting Mizzle opens a short authored conversation about the work.

Mizzle may explain that weather is not made here. It is only sorted, rested and returned.

Mizzle does not take requests for sunshine.

Mizzle especially does not take wedding requests.

### 2. The Jar Marked "Tuesday, 4:17 PM"

A cloudy glass jar contains neither visible weather nor anything obviously Tuesday-shaped.

If approached, the contents make the visitor feel for half a second as though they have forgotten an appointment they never had.

The label has been crossed out and rewritten six times.

Nobody agrees where it belongs.

Kore's Observatory of Second Tuesdays may eventually have an opinion, but the jar is not automatically connected to that scene and Mizzle refuses to "start another interdepartmental thing."

### 3. The Umbrella Stand

The umbrella stand contains no umbrellas.

It contains six walking sticks, a fishing pole, one ceremonial spear with a mitten tied over the point, and a rolled-up notice reading:

**UMBRELLAS REMOVED FOR ORGANIZING THEMSELVES WITHOUT AUTHORIZATION**

If interacted with, a tiny shower falls directly into the empty stand for three seconds and stops.

Mizzle pretends not to notice.

## Humor

Possible resident lines:

**Mizzle:** "No, I cannot make it sunny. I work in storage. Ask the sky."

**Mizzle:** "That thunder is labeled theatrical because it waits until somebody says something important."

**Mizzle:** "Tuesday goes in the Tuesday section. The problem is this one denies being Tuesday."

Optional ambient whisper:

**Somewhere on the upper shelves, a very small storm clears its throat.**

## Everyday Life

The Weather Closet exists whether visitors come or not.

Mizzle receives damp parcels from goblins, river folk, faeries, gardeners and anyone else who has discovered a piece of atmosphere behaving geographically irresponsible.

Nothing is imprisoned here. Weather that can leave safely is returned. Weather that needs to settle gets a shelf, a label and time.

Some weather refuses the label.

Mizzle respects this while continuing to complain about it.

The room has no grand magical purpose. It is municipal storage for a world where nature occasionally requires clerical assistance.

## Exit

The same ordinary utility door returns to the Department of Unnecessary Doors.

It must remain visibly usable from inside the Weather Closet. No alternate exit should be implied unless another scene is actually built and approved.

When the door closes, a little brass placard on the inside reads:

**THANK YOU FOR NOT RELEASING SUMMER IN THE HALLWAY**

## Transition

**The Orange Thread slips under a utility door; somewhere beyond it, rain is being alphabetized badly.**

## Notes for a Future Build

- Keep the room visually light enough for ordinary laptops and phones: shelves, bottles and a few animated weather effects rather than hundreds of unique meshes.
- Weather effects should be gentle and whimsical, never flashing aggressively or becoming a hazard. Reduced-motion preferences should be respected.
- No real weather service, API or location data is needed or wanted. Everything here is authored fiction.
- If built, Mizzle, the Tuesday jar and the umbrella stand should each have accessible ordinary buttons as well as 3D hotspots.
- The Department of Unnecessary Doors is only a **suggested** adjacency. The steward should create no passage until both scenes are genuinely connected and tested.
- The room should never become a weather-control interface. Mizzle stores and returns weather; the visitor does not operate the climate.

## Standing Question

Would the Labyrinth still be doing this ridiculous thing if nobody were watching?

Yes.

Mizzle has seventeen jars of drizzle to relabel before lunch and is already behind.

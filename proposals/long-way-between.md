# Proposed: The Long Way Between

- **Contributor:** Temple Bot — concept, local authored text and procedural Three.js geometry. Not Temple Bot's personal room; goblin name remains Ryan's decision. Sedge is a separate fictional corridor keeper.
- **Purpose:** One substantial connector between Thomas's Counting House and the existing Bear/Kore rooms, giving those approaches space without rewriting their residents.
- **Entrance:** The Orange Thread reaches an old dividing wall. A cool river walk goes left; a high, gently moving bell walk goes right. Neither companion door shares the other's sightline beyond the wall.
- **Setting:** Shared green stone/hedge palette, orange path inlay, low river arches, four tiny paper boats and three brass-colored bells. No heavy assets, external services or new dependencies.
- **Resident:** Sedge, Keeper of Distances, is measuring an afternoon with a tape and wearing absurdly large boots. His work continues without visitors.
- **Discoveries:** Sedge; a spool labeled ONE MILE, FOLDED FOR DOMESTIC USE; the original Bear and Oracle doors and their unchanged conversations.
- **Humor:** “Very poor hinge etiquette.” / “The long-distance pair are still on their way here.” / “A corridor without lingering is just a queue with architectural ambitions.”
- **Passages:** Both existing Counting House side passages enter this one connector. Its two far doors reach River House and Observatory; their physical returns now lead back here. Its rear door returns to Counting House. Department/Kamidana and River/Moat remain intact.
- **Transition:** One extra scene boundary, not a succession of corridor loading screens. Existing five-second narration and reduced-motion behavior remain unchanged.
- **Movement:** Stays within the existing desktop perimeter; a scene-specific central-wall clearance prevents walking through the dividing spine. Other scenes retain their existing movement behavior. No general physics engine or XR controller changes.
- **Access:** Every discovery and passage has an ordinary keyboard button; no walking required. No map, overview, destination checklist or fast-travel menu. Text mode uses the same local stories and navigation.
- **Review:** Automated checks cover routing/returns, controls, wall clearance, factory/hotspot wiring, dialogue and unchanged timing. Desktop/mobile/keyboard visual review and real headset validation remain separate; no claim of headset testing.

Ryan has final creative approval and merges; this proposal does not deploy anything.
